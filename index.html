How I got here
Grew up in Livingston, NJ. Started at Rutgers in pure statistics, liked the math but wanted to build things, so I switched into CS and Data Science. Spent the last two summers interning, first at a biotech in Cambridge, then at the Federal Reserve in Philadelphia. Graduated May 2026.
Right now
Looking for a data engineering or analyst role. I want to work somewhere with real data problems, not just dashboards. 3.5 GPA, Dean's List five times, two internships where I built things that stayed in use after I left.
Where I'm heading
Interested in the infrastructure layer: pipelines, data quality, the systems that make downstream analytics actually reliable. Still early enough in my career that I'm open to wherever good problems show up, but that's the direction I keep gravitating toward.
A bit more context

At the Fed, I built a vendor ingestion framework in Airflow and PySpark that standardized how the Economic Research Division onboards external datasets. Getting a new vendor pipeline set up used to take weeks. After the framework was in place, it took about two days. That's the kind of problem I find genuinely interesting to work on.

At Simcere I worked alongside biologists who needed to process microscopy data faster than they could manually. Batch analysis went from 10+ hours to 12 minutes. That's a pretty meaningful difference in how many experiments a team can run in a week.

Outside of work I was External VP of the Chinese Student Organization at Rutgers, which involved a lot of coordination and follow-through across a large group. I also play basketball more than is probably reasonable.

How I work
Slow to code, fast to ship
I spend time on the problem before the solution. Scope creep and wrong assumptions are way more expensive than an extra hour of planning.
Learn by building
Tutorials are fine for orientation but I don't really understand something until I've broken it. Most of my side projects started as "let me see if I can do this."
Fine with hard feedback
I'd rather find out something is wrong before it ships. Not precious about my first draft of anything.
Skeptical of frameworks
I've worked in enough codebases to appreciate when something simple would've been better. I try to reach for the right tool, not the impressive one.
Okay with not knowing
A lot of the work I've done started with no clear spec. I'm comfortable figuring it out as I go, asking questions early, and adjusting when the answer changes.
Thinks past the demo
Both internships involved handing things off to people who would actually use them. That shifts how you think about documentation, edge cases, and usability.
